This four-paper series examines how dealer balance sheet constraints propagate through repo markets and Treasury market liquidity. The unifying theme is that **intermediation capacity—not just regulatory capital—drives market functioning during stress**, and that the channels through which constraints bind are more dynamic and interconnected than previously understood.

The series covers four complementary dimensions:

1. **[Trading Desk Risk Limits and the US Treasury Liquidity Multiplier](/posts/2026/07/trading-desk-risk-limits/)** — Shows that dynamic VaR risk limits, not static capital requirements, are the binding constraint during stress. The "liquidity multiplier" creates a self-reinforcing spiral where volatility tightens risk limits, which amplifies volatility further.

2. **[Repo Netting, Central Clearing, and Dealer Balance Sheet Scarcity](/posts/2026/07/repo-netting-central-clearing/)** — Evaluates the SEC's 2023 mandatory clearing rule. Netting reduces gross balance sheet by 20.79 units, but CCP margin procyclicality erodes the benefit precisely when markets need it most.

3. **[Basis Trade Crowding, Repo Financing, and Margin Spirals](/posts/2026/07/basis-trade-crowding/)** — Quantifies the crowding-financing interaction (beta = 8.37) and decomposes amplification: 60% repo, 25% futures-cash basis, 15% dealer intermediation.

4. **[Mutual Fund Fire Sales Meet Dealer Balance Sheets](/posts/2026/07/mutual-fund-fire-sales/)** — Demonstrates that fire-sale externalities are conditional on dealer capacity. Standalone redemption effects are insignificant; only when dealers are constrained do fire sales impair liquidity (13.3 bps, t = 2.9).

Together, these papers argue for **targeting dealer intermediation capacity** as the primary lever for preserving Treasury market resilience, rather than focusing solely on capital requirements or demand-side interventions.
