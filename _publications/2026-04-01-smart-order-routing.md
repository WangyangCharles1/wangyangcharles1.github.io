We investigate whether AI-powered smart order routing improves best execution quality or creates new systemic risks. Using staggered broker adoption identification from SEC Rule 605/606 filings, we document a fundamental duality. During normal conditions, AI routers achieve 26-43 bps lower execution costs. During stress, routing concentration increases by 40-65%, creating a crowding externality. We formalize these dynamics through a multi-agent contextual bandit model and propose venue diversity requirements and correlated-routing stress tests.
