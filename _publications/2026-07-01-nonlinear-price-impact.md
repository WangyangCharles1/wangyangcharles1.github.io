We estimate the state-dependent nonlinear price impact function Lambda(Q,s) and argue it is a sufficient statistic for liquidity in venues lacking a high-frequency order book. Using Treasury auction concession as exogenous instruments, a shape-constrained neural network recovers Lambda flexibly. Applied to 1,049 auction events (2008-2024), we find that nonlinear curvature rises disproportionately when dealer constraints tighten (t=9.18), an effect invisible to linear Kyle-lambda measures.
